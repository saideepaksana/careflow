## Follow-up visits

See the pulmonology clinic within 14 days of discharge. The doctor will listen to your chest, check your oxygen level and review your inhalers.

## Medicines

Use your tiotropium inhaler once a day, at the same time each day, even when you feel well. It keeps the airways open over 24 hours and is not a rescue inhaler.

Keep using your salbutamol reliever inhaler when you feel breathless, up to 2 puffs at a time. If you need it more than four times a day, tell the nurse line.

## Oxygen readings

Check your oxygen level with the finger monitor twice a day, in the morning and evening, after sitting quietly for five minutes. Write the numbers down and bring them to your pulmonology visit.

## Warning signs

Call the nurse line the same day if your oxygen level is below 90 percent at rest.

Call 112 straight away for blue lips or new confusion.

## Activity

Walk for 10 minutes twice a day. Use pursed-lip breathing while walking: breathe in through your nose and out slowly through pursed lips. Rest whenever you need to.

## Smoke and vaccines

Do not smoke, and keep away from cooking smoke, incense and other people's smoke. Your flu vaccine is due; ask about it at your pulmonology visit.
