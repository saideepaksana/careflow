## Follow-up visits

See the cardiology clinic within 7 days of discharge. The cardiologist will check your weight, breathing and swelling, and may adjust your water tablet.

Have a basic metabolic panel blood test within 7 days of discharge. It checks your kidneys and your potassium level, which furosemide can change. Book it on or before the day of your cardiology visit so the result is ready.

## Medicines

Take furosemide 40 mg once a day, every morning. Taking it in the morning means you will not be up at night passing urine. Furosemide makes you pass more urine for about six hours after each dose. Do not stop it or change the dose unless your care team tells you to.

## Daily weight

Weigh yourself every morning after passing urine and before breakfast, wearing similar clothes, on the same scale. Write the number down each day and bring the record to your cardiology visit. Compare it with your discharge weight of 72 kg.

## Warning signs

Call the nurse line the same day if your weight goes up by more than 2 kg in a week, or if your ankles swell again.

Call 112 straight away for chest pain, or severe breathlessness at rest.

## Fluids and salt

Limit fluids to 1.5 litres a day. This includes water, tea, coffee, milk, soup, and juicy fruit such as watermelon. Using a small cup and sipping slowly helps.

Keep salt low: no added salt at the table, and avoid pickles, papad, chips, instant noodles and ready meals, which are high in salt.

## Activity

Walk for 10 to 15 minutes twice a day, and add a few minutes each week if you feel well. Stop and rest if you get breathless or dizzy. Do not lift anything heavier than 5 kg for the first two weeks.

## Driving

Do not drive until your cardiology follow-up visit confirms you are stable. Most people can drive again after that visit. Never drive if you feel dizzy or breathless.
