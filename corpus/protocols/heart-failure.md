## What heart failure means

Heart failure means the heart muscle does not pump as strongly as it should. It does not mean the heart has stopped. Fluid can build up in the lungs, making you breathless, and in the legs, making your ankles swell.

## Why daily weighing matters

A quick rise in weight is usually extra fluid, not fat. Weight often goes up a few days before breathlessness gets worse, so daily weighing gives an early warning and lets the team adjust your water tablet in time.

## Water tablets

Water tablets (diuretics) such as furosemide help the kidneys remove extra fluid. They make you pass more urine for a few hours. If you need to go out, you can ask your team whether to take the dose a little later that day.

## Exercise

Regular gentle activity such as walking strengthens the heart and muscles. Start slowly and build up. You should be able to talk while exercising. Avoid heavy lifting and straining.

## Driving

Most people with heart failure can drive a car when their symptoms are stable and they do not get breathless or dizzy at the wheel. Your cardiologist will tell you when it is safe for you.

## Alcohol

Alcohol can weaken the heart muscle and counts towards your fluid limit. Keep to small amounts, or avoid it completely if your cardiologist advises.
