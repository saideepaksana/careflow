## What a COPD flare-up is

A flare-up is when breathing gets worse than usual for several days, often after a cold or chest infection. Signs are more breathlessness, more coughing, and phlegm that is thicker or changes colour.

## Using your inhaler

Breathe out fully, away from the inhaler. Seal your lips around the mouthpiece and breathe in slowly and deeply. Hold your breath for up to 10 seconds, then breathe out slowly. For a powder inhaler such as tiotropium, breathe in quickly and deeply instead. Ask the nurse to check your technique at each visit.

## Breathing techniques

Pursed-lip breathing slows your breathing and makes it easier: breathe in through your nose for two counts and out through pursed lips for four counts. Leaning forward with your arms resting on your knees can also help when you are breathless.

## Oxygen level readings

A finger monitor measures how much oxygen is in your blood. Most people with COPD read between 92 and 96 percent. Cold fingers and nail polish can give a false low reading, so warm your hands first.

## Stopping smoking

Stopping smoking is the most effective way to slow COPD. Free support and nicotine replacement are available; ask at any clinic visit.

## Staying active

Regular walking keeps your muscles strong, so they need less oxygen. Some breathlessness while active is normal and not harmful. Pulmonary rehabilitation classes teach safe exercise; ask your pulmonologist for a referral.
