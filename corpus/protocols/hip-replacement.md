## Why hip precautions matter

In the first weeks the tissues around the new joint are still healing, and some positions can make the hip dislocate. Hip precautions, such as not bending the hip too far and not crossing your legs, protect the joint while it heals.

## Stairs

Go up with the good leg first, then the operated leg, then the walking aid. Come down with the walking aid first, then the operated leg, then the good leg. Always use the handrail.

## Preventing blood clots

After hip surgery, clots can form in the leg veins. Your blood thinner, moving your ankles up and down often, and walking a little every hour all help prevent them.

## Exercises

Do the exercises from your physiotherapist three times a day: ankle pumps, tightening your thigh muscles, and sliding the heel towards you while lying down. They restore strength and movement.

## Driving

Most people can drive about 6 weeks after a hip replacement, once they can walk without a walker and press the brake hard. Check with your surgeon first.

## Getting back to daily life

Light housework can start after a few weeks. Avoid low chairs, kneeling and heavy lifting until your surgeon clears you. Most people return to most activities by three months.
